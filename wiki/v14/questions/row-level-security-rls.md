## Answer

### Summary

Row-Level Security (RLS) in PostgreSQL 14 is a **query-rewrite feature**, not a storage or access-method feature. Policies stored in `pg_policy` are cached with the relation. During rewrite, `get_row_security_policies()` turns the applicable policies into security-barrier **`USING` quals** for existing-row visibility and **`WITH CHECK` options** for new-row validation [rowsecurity.c overview](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L1-L34). `check_enable_rls()` decides whether RLS applies, is bypassed, or must raise an error for each relation [rls.c#check_enable_rls](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/misc/rls.c#L51-L133).

RLS therefore adds rewrite, planning, and sometimes per-tuple executor work. Its `USING` quals become planned base restrictions: safely promotable clauses can drive index paths and partition pruning, while residual clauses run as scan quals [initsplan.c#process_security_barrier_quals](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/initsplan.c#L1083-L1127) [indxpath.c#match_clause_to_index](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/path/indxpath.c#L2188-L2193) [partprune.c#prune_append_rel_partitions](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/partitioning/partprune.c#L742-L804). Base-row estimates and qual costs also include those restrictions, and parallel-hazard analysis walks RLS `securityQuals`; a badly estimated or parallel-unsafe policy function can therefore change the whole plan [costsize.c#set_baserel_size_estimates](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/path/costsize.c#L4929-L4960) [planner.c#max_parallel_hazard](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/planner.c#L315-L350) [nodeFuncs.c#range_table_entry_walker](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/nodes/nodeFuncs.c#L2483-L2533). An uncorrelated scalar sublink can become a lazy InitPlan whose parameter is reused within one execution, at most once per generated InitPlan occurrence [subselect.c#build_subplan](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/subselect.c#L375-L410) [execExprInterp.c#ExecEvalParamExec](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/execExprInterp.c#L2427-L2440). Aggregates consume the already RLS-filtered input; RLS itself creates no MultiXact, although locking policy subqueries and the RLS-sensitive foreign-key validation fallback can reach tuple-lock/MultiXact paths [nodeAgg.c#ExecAgg](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/nodeAgg.c#L2145-L2188) [nodeLockRows.c#ExecLockRows](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/nodeLockRows.c#L160-L190) [ri_triggers.c#RI_Initial_Check](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/adt/ri_triggers.c#L1358-L1368).

The plan cache re-analyzes an RLS-dependent query when the effective role OID or `row_security` value differs at reuse [plancache.c#RevalidateCachedQuery](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L600-L607). PostgreSQL 14 does **not** include same-OID authorization state in that guard. Membership, `INHERIT`, `BYPASSRLS`, `SUPERUSER`, and implicit `pg_database_owner` changes can therefore leave a cached policy/bypass decision stale until another invalidation or `DISCARD PLANS` [plancache.c#InitPlanCache](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L126-L141) [acl.c#roles_is_member_of](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/adt/acl.c#L4710-L4828) [aclchk.c#has_bypassrls_privilege](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/catalog/aclchk.c#L5471-L5488) [discard.c#DISCARD-PLANS](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/discard.c#L27-L41).

The RLS-specific server GUC is `row_security`. It is `PGC_USERSET`, defaults to `on`, and has session or transaction scope; it needs neither reload nor restart [guc.c#row_security](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/misc/guc.c#L1722-L1730). The settings section separately covers the general `plan_cache_mode` and `enable_partitionwise_aggregate` GUCs, table and role attributes, policy DDL, hooks, inspection functions/views, and dump, restore, and logical-replication controls.

### How RLS is implemented

#### Catalog storage and the policy data model

Each policy is a row in the `pg_policy` system catalog. The columns are the policy name (`polname`), the owning relation (`polrelid`), the command it applies to (`polcmd`, one of the `ACL_*_CHR` characters or `'*'` for `ALL`), whether it is permissive (`polpermissive`), the roles it applies to (`polroles`, where `0` means `PUBLIC`), the `USING` expression (`polqual`), and the `WITH CHECK` expression (`polwithcheck`) [pg_policy.h#FormData_pg_policy](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/include/catalog/pg_policy.h#L29-L44). A unique index on `(polrelid, polname)` enforces unique names per table [pg_policy.h#indexes](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/include/catalog/pg_policy.h#L55-L58). The relcache builder requests that index for its scan but prepends each result with `lcons()`, so the cached policy list is the reverse of the scan order; later policy selection explicitly sorts applicable restrictive policies [policy.c#RelationBuildRowSecurity](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/policy.c#L221-L235) [policy.c#reverse-scan-order](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/policy.c#L305-L311).

Whether a table participates in RLS at all is two booleans on `pg_class`: `relrowsecurity` enables RLS and `relforcerowsecurity` requests enforcement for the table owner, both defaulting to false [pg_class.h#relrowsecurity](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/include/catalog/pg_class.h#L107-L111). Superusers and `BYPASSRLS` roles bypass independently of the force flag. For the table owner, the referential-integrity no-force context also suppresses forced RLS [rls.c#bypass-and-owner](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/misc/rls.c#L80-L118). The per-role bypass right is `pg_authid.rolbypassrls` [pg_authid.h#rolbypassrls](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/include/catalog/pg_authid.h#L41).

In memory, a loaded policy is a `RowSecurityPolicy` (name, command char, role array, permissive flag, `qual`, `with_check_qual`, and a cached `hassublinks` flag), and a relation's full policy set is a `RowSecurityDesc` holding its own memory context plus a list of policies [rowsecurity.h#RowSecurityPolicy](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/include/rewrite/rowsecurity.h#L20-L35).

#### Generated catalog/header boundary

`pg_policy.h`, `pg_class.h`, and `pg_authid.h` are catalog-definition inputs, not ordinary hand-maintained struct headers alone: each uses `CATALOG(...)`, includes a generated `*_d.h`, and states that `Catalog.pm` derives schema information from the file [pg_policy.h#catalog-input](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/include/catalog/pg_policy.h#L12-L29) [pg_class.h#catalog-input](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/include/catalog/pg_class.h#L12-L32) [pg_authid.h#catalog-input](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/include/catalog/pg_authid.h#L14-L31). The v14 catalog makefile lists `pg_policy.h` among `CATALOG_HEADERS`, derives every `*_d.h` plus `schemapg.h`, and runs `genbki.pl` to produce catalog bootstrap outputs before symlinking generated headers into the build include tree [catalog/Makefile#catalog-headers](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/catalog/Makefile#L50-L75) [catalog/Makefile#genbki](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/catalog/Makefile#L88-L118). A catalog-layout change to the RLS metadata therefore requires regeneration; runtime policy rows remain ordinary `pg_policy` data.

#### Building policies into the relcache

Relation-cache construction calls `RelationBuildRowSecurity()` whenever `pg_class.relrowsecurity` is true [relcache.c#RelationBuildDesc](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/relcache.c#L1161-L1185). The builder requests an indexed `pg_policy` scan by `polrelid`, deserializes stored `polqual` and `polwithcheck` node strings with `stringToNode()`, caches whether either expression contains a sublink, and reparents the descriptor's memory context under `CacheMemoryContext` [policy.c#RelationBuildRowSecurity](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/policy.c#L195-L325). Rewrites copy the selected cached node trees into new quals [rowsecurity.c#copy-policy-quals](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L573-L624). Catalog reads and `stringToNode()` recur only when the relcache descriptor must be rebuilt.

#### The bypass decision: `check_enable_rls`

`check_enable_rls(relid, checkAsUser, noError)` is the single decision point. It returns one of three values from `enum CheckEnableRlsResult` — `RLS_NONE`, `RLS_NONE_ENV`, `RLS_ENABLED` [rls.h#CheckEnableRlsResult](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/include/utils/rls.h#L41-L46). The logic is:

1. Built-in relations (OID `< FirstNormalObjectId`) and tables without `relrowsecurity` return `RLS_NONE` — nothing to do, and the plan need not be invalidated for RLS reasons [rls.c#check_enable_rls](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/misc/rls.c#L61-L78).
2. Superusers and roles with `BYPASSRLS` return `RLS_NONE_ENV`: RLS is skipped now, but the answer depends on the environment (the user), so the plan must be re-checked if the role changes [rls.c#bypassrls](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/misc/rls.c#L80-L88).
3. The table owner returns `RLS_NONE_ENV` unless the table has `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` set *and* this is not a referential-integrity context (`InNoForceRLSOperation()`); RI checks intentionally still bypass forced RLS for the owner [rls.c#owner](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/misc/rls.c#L90-L118).
4. Otherwise RLS applies. As a special case, if the `row_security` GUC is off and `noError` is false, it raises a "query would be affected by row-level security policy" error instead of silently filtering [rls.c#row_security-off](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/misc/rls.c#L120-L132).

`has_bypassrls_privilege()` is what step 2 consults: superusers always return true, otherwise the result is `pg_authid.rolbypassrls` [aclchk.c#has_bypassrls_privilege](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/catalog/aclchk.c#L5471-L5488). `InNoForceRLSOperation()` is true when the `SECURITY_NOFORCE_RLS` bit is set in the security context [miscinit.c#InNoForceRLSOperation](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/init/miscinit.c#L657-L661). The SQL function `row_security_active()` is just `check_enable_rls()` with `noError = true` [rls.c#row_security_active](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/misc/rls.c#L141-L167).

#### Rewrite-time expansion

RLS is applied during query rewrite, after view expansion, in `fireRIRrules()`. For each range-table entry that is a plain table or partitioned table, it calls `get_row_security_policies()` and then:

- prepends the returned security quals to the RTE's `securityQuals` list, so RLS conditions on the table take priority over barrier quals inherited from a security-barrier view [rewriteHandler.c#fireRIRrules-RLS](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rewriteHandler.c#L2256-L2349);
- concatenates the returned `WITH CHECK` options into the query's `withCheckOptions`;
- if the new quals contain sublinks, locks the referenced relations and fires RIR (view) rules on them, with recursion detection that raises "infinite recursion detected in policy for relation" [rewriteHandler.c#policy-sublinks](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rewriteHandler.c#L2285-L2336);
- marks the query `hasRowSecurity` (and `hasSubLinks` if applicable), which later drives plan-cache invalidation [rewriteHandler.c#hasRowSecurity](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rewriteHandler.c#L2351-L2358).

`get_row_security_policies()` is the core of the feature [rowsecurity.h#prototype](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/include/rewrite/rowsecurity.h#L44-L47). It only acts on `RELKIND_RELATION` and `RELKIND_PARTITIONED_TABLE` RTEs [rowsecurity.c#relkind](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L125-L128), resolves the effective user (`rte->checkAsUser` for views, else the current user) [rowsecurity.c#user_id](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L130-L134), and short-circuits on the `check_enable_rls()` result. On `RLS_NONE_ENV` it adds no quals but still sets `hasRowSecurity = true` to force re-planning if the environment changes [rowsecurity.c#RLS_NONE_ENV](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L136-L155).

The command type drives which policies are selected. If the RTE is the result relation it uses the query's command type, otherwise it treats the RTE as a `SELECT` source (so `UPDATE t1 ... FROM t2` applies t1's UPDATE policies and t2's SELECT policies) [rowsecurity.c#commandType](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L157-L169). It then layers quals for the combinations that need them:

- `SELECT ... FOR [KEY] UPDATE/SHARE` (the SELECT requires `ACL_UPDATE`) first adds the UPDATE `USING` quals, so a locking select cannot lock rows the user couldn't update [rowsecurity.c#select-for-update](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L190-L213).
- `SELECT`/`UPDATE`/`DELETE` add the command's own `USING` quals as security quals [rowsecurity.c#using-quals](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L215-L232).
- `UPDATE`/`DELETE` that also need `ACL_SELECT` (a `RETURNING` clause or a `WHERE` referencing the table) additionally add the SELECT `USING` quals [rowsecurity.c#update-needs-select](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L234-L258).
- `INSERT`/`UPDATE` add `WITH CHECK` options for new rows; if `RETURNING` requires SELECT, SELECT policies are added as `WITH CHECK` options (not security quals) so a violation errors rather than silently dropping rows [rowsecurity.c#with-check](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L260-L303).
- `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE` adds the UPDATE `USING` clauses as `WCO_RLS_CONFLICT_CHECK` and the UPDATE `WITH CHECK` clauses as update checks. When the statement requires `ACL_SELECT`, it also adds SELECT/ALL policies as both conflict checks and update checks, so an invisible conflicting or final row raises an error instead of being silently dropped [rowsecurity.c#on-conflict](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L305-L380).

Finally it copies `checkAsUser` into the new quals (for sub-selects against other relations) and sets `hasRowSecurity = true` [rowsecurity.c#finish](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L385-L397).

#### Combining policies: permissive, restrictive, and default-deny

`get_policies_for_relation()` walks the relcache policy list and keeps the policies whose `polcmd` matches the command (or is `'*'`) and that apply to the user's role, splitting them into permissive and restrictive lists. It sorts restrictive policies by name so the `WITH CHECK` options they generate are checked in a defined order; read-side quals can still be reordered by security level, leakproofness, and cost [rowsecurity.c#get_policies_for_relation](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L407-L513) [rowsecurity.c#sort_policies_by_name](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L515-L522) [createplan.c#order_qual_clauses](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/createplan.c#L5191-L5300). Extension-hook policies are appended after built-in policies. Sorting uses `strcmp` on the policy name [rowsecurity.c#row_security_policy_cmp](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L523-L545).

`add_security_quals()` builds the read-side filter: it adds each **restrictive** `USING` qual separately (implicit AND), then ORs all **permissive** `USING` quals into one expression. Crucially, **if no applicable permissive policy supplies a `USING` qual, it appends one `false` constant** — the default-deny rule that makes a row-secured table with no granting policy return nothing [rowsecurity.c#add_security_quals](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L558-L637).

`add_with_check_options()` builds the write-side check with permissive OR plus restrictive AND, using each policy's `WITH CHECK` expression or, where the command permits the fallback, its `USING` expression (the `QUAL_FOR_WCO` macro). Each restrictive check becomes its own named `WithCheckOption` so violations can name the offending policy; if no applicable permissive policy supplies a check qual, it emits one `false` check and denies the write [rowsecurity.c#add_with_check_options](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L654-L769).

#### Command and role matching

A policy applies to the current role when its role list is `PUBLIC` (`polroles[0] == ACL_ID_PUBLIC`) or `has_privs_of_role()` finds the user in one of the listed roles [rowsecurity.c#check_role_for_policy](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L775-L792). That membership expansion follows `rolinherit` and includes recursive and implicit `pg_database_owner` membership [acl.c#roles_is_member_of](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/adt/acl.c#L4710-L4828).

#### Planner: the security-barrier qual mechanism

RLS quals are placed in the RTE's `securityQuals`, the same machinery used for security-barrier views, so the planner enforces an evaluation-order rule that prevents leaking rows through cleverly-chosen user functions. Each `RestrictInfo` carries a `security_level`; a clause may not be evaluated before a clause of lower `security_level` **unless it is leakproof** [pathnodes.h#security_level](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/include/nodes/pathnodes.h#L2003-L2010). The planner computes `qual_security_level` as the maximum `securityQuals` list length across RTEs [planner.c#qual_security_level](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/planner.c#L738-L747) [pathnodes.h#qual_security_level](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/include/nodes/pathnodes.h#L343-L344), and `process_security_barrier_quals()` copies each `securityQuals` sublist into the relation's `baserestrictinfo` at successively higher security levels [initsplan.c#process_security_barrier_quals](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/initsplan.c#L1083-L1127).

At plan-build time, `order_qual_clauses()` sorts the quals at each node: lower security level first, and within a level by estimated cost. A leakproof clause that is also cheap (under `10 * cpu_operator_cost`) is treated as security level 0 so it can be evaluated early; everything else keeps its real level [createplan.c#order_qual_clauses](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/createplan.c#L5191-L5300).

#### Executor: enforcing `WITH CHECK`

`USING` quals become planned base restrictions. Securely promotable restrictions can become index quals or pruning quals; residual restrictions are executor scan quals, and all visibility restrictions silently exclude rows [initsplan.c#process_security_barrier_quals](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/initsplan.c#L1070-L1127) [indxpath.c#match_clause_to_index](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/path/indxpath.c#L2188-L2193) [partprune.c#prune_append_rel_partitions](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/partitioning/partprune.c#L742-L804). `WITH CHECK` options are enforced separately by `ExecWithCheckOptions()`, which evaluates each option of the requested kind against the new tuple and raises an error if it returns NULL or false. `ExecInsert()` selects INSERT or UPDATE checks (the latter for cross-partition row movement), `ExecUpdate()` selects UPDATE checks, and the `ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE` path selects conflict checks before calling `ExecUpdate()` [nodeModifyTable.c#ExecInsert-RLS](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/nodeModifyTable.c#L894-L917) [nodeModifyTable.c#ExecUpdate-RLS](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/nodeModifyTable.c#L1880-L1901) [nodeModifyTable.c#ON-CONFLICT-RLS](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/nodeModifyTable.c#L2322-L2332). For RLS kinds, `ExecWithCheckOptions()` reports "new row violates row-level security policy [\"name\"] for table" and, unlike view checks, deliberately does **not** include the failing row's data, because the user might not be allowed to see that row [execMain.c#ExecWithCheckOptions](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/execMain.c#L1977-L2105). Permission checks (`ExecCheckRTPerms`) are explicitly separate from RLS; callers that return rows must also consult `check_enable_rls()` [execMain.c#ExecCheckRTPerms](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/execMain.c#L548-L560).

#### Plan-cache invalidation

Because the policies that apply depend on the current role and the `row_security` GUC, cached plans for RLS queries are environment-sensitive [plancache.c#overview](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L16-L20). The rewriter's `hasRowSecurity` flag is harvested into the plan source's `dependsOnRLS` field via `extract_query_dependencies()` (which reuses the `dependsOnRole` slot as a collector) [setrefs.c#extract_query_dependencies](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/setrefs.c#L3142-L3167) [setrefs.c#hasRowSecurity](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/setrefs.c#L3219-L3221), alongside the role and `row_security` value captured at rewrite time [plancache.c#capture](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L396-L403). `RevalidateCachedQuery()` then forces a re-analysis/re-plan if `dependsOnRLS` is set and either the user id or the `row_security` setting has changed [plancache.c#RevalidateCachedQuery](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L600-L607). The built plan is likewise marked role-dependent [plancache.c#BuildCachedPlan](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L982-L994), and the fast "simple validity" revalidation path is disabled for RLS-dependent plans [plancache.c#simple-validity](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L1343-L1345).

#### Same-role authorization-state cache boundary

The v14 RLS plan-cache guard records only the effective role OID and `row_security`; it has no authorization-generation field [plancache.h#RLS-cache-fields](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/include/utils/plancache.h#L110-L127). That is narrower than the inputs to RLS. Policy matching calls `has_privs_of_role()`, whose recursive expansion depends on role grants, `rolinherit`, and implicit membership in `pg_database_owner`; bypass additionally depends on `rolsuper` and `rolbypassrls` [rowsecurity.c#check_role_for_policy](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L771-L792) [acl.c#roles_is_member_of](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/adt/acl.c#L4710-L4828) [acl.c#has_privs_of_role](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/adt/acl.c#L4852-L4876) [aclchk.c#has_bypassrls_privilege](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/catalog/aclchk.c#L5471-L5488).

Authorization catalog invalidations clear the ACL membership caches for `pg_auth_members`, `pg_authid`, and `pg_database`, but `InitPlanCache()` registers no callback for those caches [acl.c#RoleMembershipCacheCallback](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/adt/acl.c#L4654-L4707) [plancache.c#InitPlanCache](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L126-L141). Consequently, an unchanged effective role OID can retain a stale rewritten RLS tree and generic plan after a relevant role-membership grant/revoke, `INHERIT`/`NOINHERIT`, `BYPASSRLS`/`NOBYPASSRLS`, `SUPERUSER`/`NOSUPERUSER`, intermediate-role inheritance change, or database-owner change [plancache.c#RevalidateCachedQuery](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L600-L607). A fresh query rebuilds policy selection after the ACL callback clears its caches, but a prepared or procedural cached statement can retain the old policy set or bypass decision. Revoking membership or bypass is the dangerous direction because an old tree can continue granting rows. Recycle affected sessions or run `DISCARD PLANS`, which calls `ResetPlanCache()` [discard.c#DISCARD-PLANS](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/discard.c#L27-L41) [plancache.c#ResetPlanCache](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L2151-L2195).

#### Inheritance and partitioning

During a query through an inheritance or partitioned parent, child tables do **not** contribute their own policies. The planner deliberately clears each expanded child RTE's `securityQuals`, then translates the parent's base restrictions for each child [inherit.c#expand_single_inheritance_child](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/util/inherit.c#L457-L487) [inherit.c#apply_child_basequals](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/util/inherit.c#L831-L952). The parent's policies therefore govern rows reached through the parent; a direct query against a child applies that child's own RLS configuration [ddl.sgml#inheritance](../../../raw/postgres-14/doc/src/sgml/ddl.sgml#L3451-L3463) [rowsecurity.sql#partition-child-RLS](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/test/regress/sql/rowsecurity.sql#L382-L402).

#### Views and policy execution identity

Policy expressions normally run with the privileges of the user running the query, so that user must be able to access referenced functions and relations [create_policy.sgml#policy-rights](../../../raw/postgres-14/doc/src/sgml/ref/create_policy.sgml#L670-L680). A `SECURITY DEFINER` function instead runs as its owner [create_function.sgml#SECURITY-DEFINER](../../../raw/postgres-14/doc/src/sgml/ref/create_function.sgml#L405-L415). Views are another identity boundary in PostgreSQL 14: the stored rule records the view owner, view expansion installs that identity as `RangeTblEntry.checkAsUser`, and `get_row_security_policies()` uses it for underlying-table policies [rewriteDefine.c#view-owner](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rewriteDefine.c#L806-L852) [rewriteHandler.c#checkAsUser](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rewriteHandler.c#L1906-L1920) [rowsecurity.c#user_id](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L130-L134) [rowsecurity.sql#view-owner-policies](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/test/regress/sql/rowsecurity.sql#L871-L919).

#### Feature boundaries (COPY, CTAS, whole-table operations, RI, replication, and errors)

Several subsystems special-case RLS:

- **COPY**: when RLS applies, `COPY TO table` becomes query-based `COPY (SELECT ...)`, and the generated source is marked `ONLY` so inheritance children are not added. `COPY FROM` is rejected with "COPY FROM not supported with row-level security" and directs the user to `INSERT` [copy.c#RLS](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/copy.c#L201-L227) [copy.c#RLS-ONLY](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/copy.c#L283-L300).
- **CREATE TABLE AS / SELECT INTO**: for a data-producing command, the source `SELECT` goes through ordinary rewrite and planning, so its source tables retain their RLS restrictions [createas.c#QueryRewrite](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/createas.c#L285-L315). The destination receiver separately errors with "policies not yet implemented for this command" if RLS would apply to the newly constructed destination relation [createas.c#RLS](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/createas.c#L535-L546).
- **Whole-table operations**: RLS governs row-returning and row-modifying DML, not operations such as `TRUNCATE` or the `REFERENCES` privilege that apply to the table as a whole [ddl.sgml#whole-table-boundary](../../../raw/postgres-14/doc/src/sgml/ddl.sgml#L2243-L2252).
- **Referential integrity**: unique, primary-key, and foreign-key integrity checks bypass RLS, which preserves integrity but can create covert channels [ddl.sgml#RI-bypass](../../../raw/postgres-14/doc/src/sgml/ddl.sgml#L2543-L2548). Separately, initial foreign-key validation skips its one-query bulk path when the validating role lacks `BYPASSRLS` and does not own every RLS-enabled participating table; the fallback scans the referencing table and invokes the row trigger check for each tuple [ri_triggers.c#RI_Initial_Check](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/adt/ri_triggers.c#L1358-L1368) [tablecmds.c#validateForeignKeyConstraint](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/tablecmds.c#L11367-L11415).
- **Logical replication**: initial table synchronization issues `COPY table TO STDOUT` on the publisher, so the publisher connection can enter the RLS-aware `COPY TO` path [tablesync.c#copy_table](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/replication/logical/tablesync.c#L836-L890) [copy.c#RLS](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/copy.c#L201-L227). The v14 docs recommend publisher connection option `options=-crow_security=off` when table owners are untrusted, making RLS applicability stop the copy rather than filter rows or execute an untrusted policy [logical-replication.sgml#security](../../../raw/postgres-14/doc/src/sgml/logical-replication.sgml#L533-L562). Ongoing `pgoutput` streaming instead serializes decoded WAL changes according to publication action flags; it does not run each change through a table query and its policy quals [pgoutput.c#pgoutput_change](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/replication/pgoutput/pgoutput.c#L573-L680).
- **Error-message leakage**: index-key, row, partition-key, and RI error-detail builders normally suppress protected values when RLS is active [genam.c#BuildIndexValueDescription](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/access/index/genam.c#L192-L208) [execMain.c#ExecBuildSlotValueDescription](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/execMain.c#L2142-L2148) [execPartition.c#partition-key-desc](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/execPartition.c#L1362-L1365) [ri_triggers.c#error-detail](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/adt/ri_triggers.c#L2440-L2456). The RI builder treats a detached or dropped referenced partition (`partgone`) as permission-safe and skips those RLS/privilege checks [ri_triggers.c#partgone](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/adt/ri_triggers.c#L2440-L2456).

#### Extension hooks

Two global hooks let an extension inject policies at rewrite time: `row_security_policy_hook_permissive` (combined with OR, like other permissive policies) and `row_security_policy_hook_restrictive` (combined with AND, name-sorted, after built-in restrictive policies) [rowsecurity.h#hooks](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/include/rewrite/rowsecurity.h#L37-L42) [rowsecurity.c#hook-decls](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L87-L97) [rowsecurity.c#hook-application](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L473-L512). The hooks run only after relkind validation and `check_enable_rls()` returns `RLS_ENABLED`; they cannot enable RLS or override owner, superuser, or `BYPASSRLS` decisions [rowsecurity.c#RLS-short-circuits](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L125-L155). The in-tree example and coverage live under `src/test/modules/test_rls_hooks`, whose `_PG_init()` saves the previous hook pointers and installs both callbacks [test_rls_hooks.c#hook-installation](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/test/modules/test_rls_hooks/test_rls_hooks.c#L30-L58) [test_rls_hooks.c#policy-builders](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/test/modules/test_rls_hooks/test_rls_hooks.c#L60-L180).

### Scalability and performance issues

RLS adds work at rewrite, planning, and execution, but the execution frequency is expression-dependent rather than uniformly "once per row." The main source-visible concerns are:

1. **Tuple-level checks.** Residual `USING` scan quals run for each tuple that reaches the qual, and `WITH CHECK` expressions run for each candidate inserted or updated tuple [execScan.c#ExecScan](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/execScan.c#L202-L234) [execMain.c#ExecWithCheckOptions](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/execMain.c#L1977-L2105). A function inside such a filter is called per evaluation, but an immutable call with plan-time-constant inputs may fold, each eligible uncorrelated scalar sublink may become its own InitPlan, and a non-volatile comparison value in an index qual may become a runtime key [clauses.c#eval_const_expressions](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/util/clauses.c#L2135-L2139) [subselect.c#build_subplan](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/subselect.c#L375-L410) [nodeIndexscan.c#ExecReScanIndexScan](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/nodeIndexscan.c#L554-L572).

2. **Security ordering can block index quals.** A higher-level non-leakproof user qual cannot become an index qual ahead of a lower-security-level RLS restriction; `restriction_is_securely_promotable()` admits it only when its security level is safe or the clause is leakproof [indxpath.c#match_clause_to_index](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/path/indxpath.c#L2188-L2193) [restrictinfo.c#restriction_is_securely_promotable](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/util/restrictinfo.c#L396-L409). At execution-plan construction, only cheap leakproof clauses can be treated as level 0 and moved early [createplan.c#order_qual_clauses](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/createplan.c#L5191-L5300). `LEAKPROOF` promises no argument disclosure through side effects or errors and is superuser-only, so it must not be used merely as a performance hint [create_function.sgml#LEAKPROOF](../../../raw/postgres-14/doc/src/sgml/ref/create_function.sgml#L356-L378) [functioncmds.c#leakproof-check](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/functioncmds.c#L1157-L1165).

3. **Policy subqueries add planning, execution, and concurrency costs.** A policy sublink makes rewrite walk it, lock referenced relations, and recursively apply RIR rules [policy.c#hassublinks](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/policy.c#L301-L303) [rewriteHandler.c#policy-sublinks](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rewriteHandler.c#L2283-L2336). A correlated subquery remains a `SubPlan` that can run at each outer tuple where the expression is evaluated; an uncorrelated scalar subquery can instead become an on-demand InitPlan whose result is reused for that occurrence during the execution [subselect.c#build_subplan](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/subselect.c#L375-L410) [subselect.c#correlated-SubPlan](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/subselect.c#L487-L539). The v14 docs call current-row-only expressions the simplest and best-performing case, warn that cross-row lookups can race, and note that the `FOR SHARE` mitigation can itself become expensive under heavy concurrency and frequent updates [ddl.sgml#RLS-policy-lookups](../../../raw/postgres-14/doc/src/sgml/ddl.sgml#L2563-L2572) [ddl.sgml#RLS-row-share-lock](../../../raw/postgres-14/doc/src/sgml/ddl.sgml#L2658-L2675).

4. **Many policies enlarge rewrite and qual work.** Each RLS-applicable rewrite scans the relation's cached policy list, tests command and role applicability, sorts the applicable restrictive policies by name, and copies restrictive quals plus the OR-combined permissive quals [rowsecurity.c#get_policies_for_relation](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L407-L513) [rowsecurity.c#add_security_quals](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L558-L637). Role matching walks each policy's role array and calls `has_privs_of_role()`; recursive membership expansion is then cached within the backend [rowsecurity.c#check_role_for_policy](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L775-L792) [acl.c#membership-cache](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/adt/acl.c#L4735-L4757) [acl.c#cache-result](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/adt/acl.c#L4831-L4848). The sort happens during policy selection for a rewrite, not during relcache construction.

5. **Plan-cache churn — and a same-role authorization blind spot.** Prepared statements and PL/pgSQL plans for RLS queries are re-analyzed when the effective role OID or `row_security` differs at reuse, and they cannot use the cheap simple-validity path [plancache.c#RevalidateCachedQuery](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L600-L607) [plancache.c#simple-validity](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L1338-L1348). A pool that changes roles can therefore pay repeated rewrite and planning costs. Conversely, same-OID changes to membership, `INHERIT`, `BYPASSRLS`, `SUPERUSER`, or implicit database-owner membership do not change either RLS cache-key field, and the plan cache has no auth/database callback [plancache.h#RLS-cache-fields](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/include/utils/plancache.h#L110-L127) [acl.c#RoleMembershipCacheCallback](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/adt/acl.c#L4654-L4707) [plancache.c#InitPlanCache](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L126-L141). [RLS and the plan cache](#rls-and-the-plan-cache) details both boundaries.

6. **Some statements layer policy sets.** `SELECT ... FOR [KEY] UPDATE/SHARE` adds UPDATE `USING` policies before SELECT policies. `UPDATE`/`DELETE` statements that require SELECT rights add SELECT policies too, and write paths may add both visibility and new-row checks [rowsecurity.c#select-for-update](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L190-L258) [rowsecurity.c#with-check](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L260-L303).

7. **Foreign-key validation can lose the bulk path.** If the validating role neither bypasses RLS nor owns every RLS-enabled participating table, the one-query FK validation path is skipped and every referencing tuple calls `RI_FKey_check_ins()` [ri_triggers.c#RI_Initial_Check](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/adt/ri_triggers.c#L1358-L1368) [tablecmds.c#validateForeignKeyConstraint](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/tablecmds.c#L11367-L11415). Non-null checks that reach a matching referenced row use `FOR KEY SHARE`, so hot keys can also incur the MultiXact work described below [ri_triggers.c#RI_FKey_check](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/adt/ri_triggers.c#L330-L390).

8. **Default deny is cheap but easy to misdiagnose.** Without an applicable permissive policy, rewrite adds a constant-false visibility qual and, for a new-row check, a false `WITH CHECK` option [rowsecurity.c#default-deny](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L626-L636) [rowsecurity.c#default-deny-WCO](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L750-L768). The planner can exclude a base relation whose sole restriction is false. `SELECT` returns no rows and `UPDATE`/`DELETE` normally find no targets, while `INSERT` or another new-row check raises an RLS violation [plancat.c#constant-false-exclusion](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/util/plancat.c#L1516-L1534) [execMain.c#ExecWithCheckOptions](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/execMain.c#L2010-L2085) [rowsecurity.sql#default-deny-DML](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/test/regress/sql/rowsecurity.sql#L1518-L1531).

9. **Partition fan-out duplicates planning structures.** The planner translates and copies parent base restrictions to each surviving child relation [inherit.c#apply_child_basequals](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/util/inherit.c#L831-L952). More surviving partitions therefore mean more child planning structures carrying those quals. Executor work follows the tuples, rescans, and lossy rechecks that actually evaluate each child's remaining quals, not a fixed multiplier based only on partition count [execScan.c#ExecScan](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/execScan.c#L202-L234) [nodeBitmapHeapscan.c#lossy-recheck](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/nodeBitmapHeapscan.c#L314-L328).

10. **RLS DDL takes strong locks.** `CREATE POLICY`, `ALTER POLICY`, policy rename, and policy drop acquire `AccessExclusiveLock` on the table, and all four `ALTER TABLE ... ROW LEVEL SECURITY` forms select the same lock mode [policy.c#CreatePolicy-lock](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/policy.c#L631-L638) [policy.c#AlterPolicy-lock](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/policy.c#L808-L814) [policy.c#RemovePolicyById-lock](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/policy.c#L364-L399) [policy.c#rename_policy-lock](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/policy.c#L1113-L1119) [tablecmds.c#RLS-lock-mode](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/tablecmds.c#L4266-L4284). `ALTER TABLE` keeps its selected lock through commit, and `AccessExclusiveLock` conflicts with every standard table lock mode, so policy deployment can block concurrent table use for the DDL transaction [tablecmds.c#ATPrepCmd-lock-lifetime](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/tablecmds.c#L4471-L4477) [lock.c#LockConflicts](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/storage/lmgr/lock.c#L65-L105).

11. **Policy selectivity and cost affect the wider plan.** The planner estimates base rows from the complete `baserestrictinfo` and charges the cost of those quals [costsize.c#set_baserel_size_estimates](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/path/costsize.c#L4929-L4960). A bare Boolean policy-function clause uses its planner support function for selectivity when one exists; otherwise PostgreSQL 14 falls back to `0.3333333`, which can distort row counts and downstream join or aggregate choices [clausesel.c#function-selectivity](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/path/clausesel.c#L890-L904) [plancat.c#function_selectivity](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/util/plancat.c#L1935-L1990).

12. **Policy functions can constrain parallel plans.** The planner computes the query's maximum parallel hazard before path construction [planner.c#max_parallel_hazard](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/planner.c#L315-L350). Its query walker descends into each RTE's `securityQuals`, so a parallel-unsafe policy function disables parallel mode and a parallel-restricted function is recorded as a restriction [nodeFuncs.c#range_table_entry_walker](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/nodes/nodeFuncs.c#L2483-L2533) [clauses.c#parallel-hazard](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/util/clauses.c#L675-L778).

### Do RLS filter conditions need to be in the query?

**No. PostgreSQL 14 already gives the planner every applicable policy `USING` expression. You do not need to copy the complete effective RLS condition into `WHERE` to make it visible to planning.** Rewrite prepends the RLS expressions to the range-table entry's `securityQuals` [rewriteHandler.c#fireRIRrules-RLS](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rewriteHandler.c#L2338-L2349). `process_security_barrier_quals()` then transfers them into the table's `baserestrictinfo`, giving the first security sublist level 0 and later sublists higher levels [initsplan.c#process_security_barrier_quals](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/initsplan.c#L1083-L1127). Index paths, partial-index implication, base-row estimates, and partition pruning consume that restriction list [indxpath.c#create_index_paths](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/path/indxpath.c#L198-L336) [indxpath.c#check_index_predicates](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/path/indxpath.c#L3306-L3421) [costsize.c#set_baserel_size_estimates](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/path/costsize.c#L4929-L4960) [partprune.c#prune_append_rel_partitions](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/partitioning/partprune.c#L742-L804).

#### The planner already uses the policy qual

Two planner paths consume the RLS qual directly, with no help from the query text:

- **Index scans.** `match_clause_to_index()` uses a base-restriction clause as an index qualifier unless `restriction_is_securely_promotable()` rejects it [indxpath.c#match_clause_to_index](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/path/indxpath.c#L2188-L2193). That helper accepts a clause whose level is no greater than `baserestrict_min_security`, or one that is leakproof [restrictinfo.c#restriction_is_securely_promotable](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/util/restrictinfo.c#L396-L409). The first RLS sublist is level 0. In a permissive-only policy set, the permissive expression occupies that first sublist; preprocessing can split top-level AND terms, but they remain at level 0 [rowsecurity.c#add_security_quals](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L558-L637) [planner.c#securityQuals-preprocess](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/planner.c#L931-L941) [initsplan.c#process_security_barrier_quals](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/initsplan.c#L1088-L1123). The planner can therefore match structurally suitable policy clauses to indexes without a query-text copy.
- **Partition pruning.** Plan-time pruning takes its prune quals from `rel->baserestrictinfo`, before child expansion [partprune.c#prune_append_rel_partitions](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/partitioning/partprune.c#L742-L804) [inherit.c#expand_inherited_rtentry](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/util/inherit.c#L345-L417). A policy predicate on the partition key can therefore prune partitions without being restated; the RLS regression expected output includes that plan shape [rowsecurity.out#RLS-pruning](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/test/regress/expected/rowsecurity.out#L1059-L1067).

#### Restating the complete effective predicate is redundant

Quals from the query text receive the *maximum* security level, `root->qual_security_level` [planner.c#qual_security_level](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/planner.c#L738-L747) [initsplan.c#query-quals](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/initsplan.c#L817-L838). An exact copy of the complete effective RLS condition therefore lands above every policy security sublist and is securely promotable only if it is leakproof [restrictinfo.c#restriction_is_securely_promotable](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/util/restrictinfo.c#L396-L409). It adds no new logical restriction and can leave duplicate expression work.

Do not confuse that with restating **one branch** of an effective permissive-policy OR. PostgreSQL combines permissive policies as `P1 OR P2 ...`; adding `WHERE P1` changes the result to `(P1 OR P2) AND P1`, which is equivalent to `P1`, not to the original RLS condition [rowsecurity.c#permissive-OR](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L569-L624). That stronger user filter can expose `P1` as a separate index or pruning clause, but the speedup comes with narrower SQL semantics.

#### The real constraint is your *other* filters

The design rule is that a lower-`security_level` qual must be evaluated before a higher one unless the higher one is leakproof [optimizer README#security-level-rules](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/README#L910-L969) [pathnodes.h#security_level](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/include/nodes/pathnodes.h#L2003-L2010). Your own selective `WHERE` predicate therefore cannot be pushed to an index ahead of the RLS filter unless it is leakproof, and `order_qual_clauses()` enforces the same ordering at execution time (only a cheap leakproof clause is treated as level 0) [createplan.c#order_qual_clauses](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/createplan.c#L5191-L5300). This is the leakproof-ordering trap from [Scalability and performance issues](#scalability-and-performance-issues) (item 2), seen from the query author's side.

So the levers that actually help planning are not about *where you write* the condition:

1. **Do not duplicate the complete effective policy condition as an optimization.** The planner already sees it as base restrictions at assigned security levels; an exact query copy arrives at the maximum level and adds no logical information [rewriteHandler.c#fireRIRrules-RLS](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rewriteHandler.c#L2338-L2349) [initsplan.c#query-quals](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/initsplan.c#L817-L838).
2. **Make policy predicates structurally indexable and useful for pruning.** The planner can match safely promotable restrictions to indexes, use RLS restrictions to help prove a partial-index predicate, and prune from the same base-restriction list. If the predicate prover cannot establish an implication, the RLS clause merely fails to help; adding restrictions does not make an already-proven partial index cease to be `predOK` [indxpath.c#match_clause_to_index](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/path/indxpath.c#L2188-L2193) [indxpath.c#check_index_predicates](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/path/indxpath.c#L3306-L3421) [partprune.c#prune_append_rel_partitions](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/partitioning/partprune.c#L742-L804).
3. **Use only genuinely leakproof query filters when early evaluation matters.** Leakproofness can allow a higher-level query qual to move early, but it is a superuser-set security property, not a tuning hint [create_function.sgml#LEAKPROOF](../../../raw/postgres-14/doc/src/sgml/ref/create_function.sgml#L356-L378).

Two boundaries matter. First, `add_security_quals()` appends restrictive policies before the OR-combined permissive qual. Security levels then rise in list order: with mixed policies, the first restrictive qual is level 0 and the permissive visibility expression is later; with only permissive policies, that combined visibility expression is level 0 [rowsecurity.c#add_security_quals](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L599-L624) [initsplan.c#process_security_barrier_quals](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/initsplan.c#L1088-L1123). Layered command-policy sets add still more sublists, and only the minimum-level clauses are unconditionally promotable [rowsecurity.c#select-for-update](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L190-L258). Second, `WITH CHECK` options are write-side executor checks, not index or pruning quals, so restating one in the query does not turn that WCO into a planning restriction [execMain.c#ExecWithCheckOptions](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/execMain.c#L1977-L2105).

### Wrapping a function in a subquery to evaluate it once

**Yes, when the unwrapped call would otherwise remain in a repeatedly evaluated filter. Wrapping an uncorrelated function call in a scalar sub-SELECT — `(SELECT auth.uid()) = user_id` instead of `auth.uid() = user_id` — can make PostgreSQL 14 evaluate each generated InitPlan occurrence on first demand at most once per query execution.** The planner turns each eligible uncorrelated scalar sublink in the rewritten tree into an *InitPlan* whose result is stored in an executor parameter (`PARAM_EXEC`) and reused after first demand [subselect.c#process_sublinks_mutator](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/subselect.c#L1922-L1950) [subselect.c#build_subplan](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/subselect.c#L396-L410). A `STABLE` function expression that remains in a filter after simplification is evaluated whenever that filter step runs, although a simple SQL wrapper may inline and a structurally indexable non-volatile comparison value may instead become a runtime key [clauses.c#simplify_function](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/util/clauses.c#L3885-L3973) [execScan.c#ExecScan](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/execScan.c#L202-L234) [indxpath.c#match_opclause_to_indexcol](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/path/indxpath.c#L2483-L2543). This is general planner/executor behavior and applies inside policy `USING`/`WITH CHECK` expressions because the planner preprocesses their sublinks too [planner.c#securityQuals-preprocess](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/planner.c#L931-L941) [planner.c#withCheckOptions-preprocess](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/planner.c#L818-L828).

A note on the functions: `auth.uid()` and `auth.jwt()` are external helper functions, not part of core PostgreSQL 14, so the pinned `raw/postgres-14/` checkout cannot verify their definitions or volatility. The PostgreSQL mechanism described here is source-verified for any uncorrelated scalar sub-SELECT. If a JWT helper is implemented as a claim reader over a session setting and declared `STABLE`, it has the same volatility class as core `current_setting()` (`provolatile => 's'`) [pg_proc.dat#current_setting](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/include/catalog/pg_proc.dat#L6009-L6015). If a helper is declared `VOLATILE`, the uncorrelated sub-SELECT still follows the InitPlan rule below, but that intentionally changes the number of calls compared with a bare per-row filter; verify that at-most-once, first-demand semantics are correct before using the wrapper.

#### The mechanism: an uncorrelated sub-SELECT becomes an InitPlan

`(SELECT auth.uid())` is a scalar sub-SELECT that references no outer-row column: an uncorrelated `EXPR_SUBLINK`. `SS_process_sublinks()` calls `make_subplan()` for every encountered `SubLink` [subselect.c#process_sublinks_mutator](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/subselect.c#L1922-L1950). For each `EXPR_SUBLINK` with no correlation parameters (`parParam == NIL`), `build_subplan()` allocates a fresh `PARAM_EXEC`, records it in `setParam`, marks the subplan as an InitPlan, and replaces that occurrence with the parameter [subselect.c#build_subplan](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/subselect.c#L375-L410). The subplan is attached to the query level's InitPlan list [subselect.c#init_plans](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/subselect.c#L548-L556). Thus one occurrence of `user_id = (SELECT auth.uid())` becomes a comparison to one InitPlan parameter. Two written occurrences can become two InitPlans; PostgreSQL does not establish a function-global common-subexpression cache here.

At execution time one InitPlan occurrence runs lazily at most once; if its output is never demanded, it need not run. `ExecEvalParamExec()` invokes it only while the parameter's `execPlan` field remains set, and `ExecSetParamPlan()` clears that field after storing the result [execExprInterp.c#ExecEvalParamExec](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/execExprInterp.c#L2427-L2440) [nodeSubplan.c#ExecSetParamPlan](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/nodeSubplan.c#L1051-L1184). Scalar-subquery semantics still apply: no row yields NULL, while a second row raises a cardinality violation [nodeSubplan.c#scalar-cardinality](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/nodeSubplan.c#L1152-L1158) [nodeSubplan.c#empty-scalar-result](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/nodeSubplan.c#L1207-L1230).

#### Without the wrapper: a remaining `STABLE` filter expression is per evaluation

A `STABLE` or `VOLATILE` function is not pre-evaluated as a constant during ordinary planning; that path accepts only `IMMUTABLE` functions [clauses.c#eval_const_expressions](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/util/clauses.c#L2135-L2139) [clauses.c#ece_function_is_safe](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/util/clauses.c#L3577-L3591). A simple SQL-language wrapper may instead be inlined, so the outer helper call need not remain literally as a `FuncExpr`; after simplification, any remaining function expression in a scan filter is still executor work rather than a cross-row memoized value [clauses.c#simplify_function](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/util/clauses.c#L3885-L3973).

When that filter qual runs, the scan loop places each fetched tuple in the expression context and calls `ExecQual()` [execScan.c#ExecScan](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/execScan.c#L202-L234). Each `EEOP_FUNCEXPR` step invokes its function for that evaluation, with no cross-row memoization in the expression step [execExprInterp.c#EEOP_FUNCEXPR](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/execExprInterp.c#L727-L735). Thus the unwrapped expression's remaining function work can be per candidate row; the wrapper avoids that only when it changes the plan to the uncorrelated InitPlan shape above.

#### Why it matters for RLS policies specifically

RLS `USING` expressions become scan-level security quals during rewrite and are transferred into `baserestrictinfo` (see [Do RLS filter conditions need to be in the query?](#do-rls-filter-conditions-need-to-be-in-the-query)). If the policy comparison becomes an exact, non-lossy index condition, its non-constant side can be a runtime key computed when the scan initializes or rescans [nodeIndexscan.c#ExecReScanIndexScan](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/nodeIndexscan.c#L542-L572). In that shape, a bare non-volatile helper may already have little per-row cost. A lossy index scan or lossy bitmap page rechecks the original index expression against fetched tuples, so the bare helper can run again; an InitPlan parameter remains a parameter during that recheck [nodeIndexscan.c#lossy-recheck](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/nodeIndexscan.c#L130-L150) [nodeBitmapHeapscan.c#lossy-recheck](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/nodeBitmapHeapscan.c#L314-L328).

The clearest win is when the policy predicate is a **filter** rather than an exact index condition: no usable index, a sequential scan, a lossy recheck, or another shape the planner cannot convert. Any remaining function step can then run for each candidate tuple reaching it, while the `(SELECT ...)` occurrence reduces the helper call to at most one on-demand InitPlan evaluation. Wrapping also turns the compared value into a parameter (`user_id = $0`). Index matching still requires a safely promotable qual, indexed-operand shape, no same-relation variable on the other side, no volatile expression there, compatible collation, and an operator-family member or support-function match [restrictinfo.c#restriction_is_securely_promotable](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/util/restrictinfo.c#L396-L409) [indxpath.c#match_opclause_to_indexcol](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/path/indxpath.c#L2483-L2543).

#### Caveats and limits

- **Caching is per occurrence and per execution, not general memoization.** The parameter is captured on first demand and reused for that occurrence [nodeSubplan.c#ExecSetParamPlan](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/nodeSubplan.c#L1051-L1184). Separate occurrences, including copies placed in different policy checks, can create separate InitPlans; a later statement execution gets new parameter state [subselect.c#process_sublinks_mutator](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/subselect.c#L1922-L1950).
- **Only uncorrelated sub-SELECTs get this InitPlan shape.** `(SELECT auth.uid())` references no outer column. A correlated sub-SELECT retains `parParam != NIL` and is built as a `SubPlan`; it can run for every outer tuple at which the expression is evaluated, subject to qual short-circuiting and rescans [subselect.c#build_subplan-SubPlan](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/subselect.c#L487-L539).
- **An InitPlan has planning consequences.** PostgreSQL adds its startup and per-call cost to final paths, marks those final paths parallel-unsafe, and removes final partial paths [subselect.c#SS_charge_for_initplans](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/subselect.c#L2113-L2167). That does not categorically eliminate every parallel node: the regression suite shows an InitPlan with `Gather` nodes and a parameter evaluated for workers [select_parallel.out#parallel-InitPlan](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/test/regress/expected/select_parallel.out#L327-L349) [execParallel.c#InitPlan-parameters](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/execParallel.c#L613-L624).
- **Wrapping adds rewrite tree walks.** A policy sub-SELECT sets `hassublinks`, so rewrite enters its sublink-lock and RIR-rule traversal [policy.c#hassublinks](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/policy.c#L301-L303) [rewriteHandler.c#policy-sublinks](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rewriteHandler.c#L2283-L2336). A relation-free `(SELECT auth.uid())` gives that walker no referenced relation to lock, but the extra traversal and later subquery planning still occur.
- **This is orthogonal to restating the predicate in the query.** [Do RLS filter conditions need to be in the query?](#do-rls-filter-conditions-need-to-be-in-the-query) is about *where* the condition lives; this is about *how the function call is written*. You still do not need to restate the policy in your query, and when you do reference such a function in your own query predicates, the same `(SELECT ...)` trick applies there.

### RLS and aggregation

Yes, RLS can slow aggregation statements, but not because PostgreSQL 14 has a separate RLS-aware aggregate executor. RLS adds `USING` expressions as scan-level security quals during rewrite [rowsecurity.c overview](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L1-L34), the planner transfers those `securityQuals` into the base relation's `baserestrictinfo` [initsplan.c#process_security_barrier_quals](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/initsplan.c#L1070-L1127), and aggregate paths sit above a chosen input subpath [pathnode.c#create_agg_path](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/util/pathnode.c#L3100-L3155). At execution time, `ExecAgg` receives tuples from its outer subplan and advances aggregate state for each input tuple [nodeAgg.c#ExecAgg](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/nodeAgg.c#L2145-L2188) [nodeAgg.c#agg-input-loop](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/nodeAgg.c#L2360-L2465). So `COUNT(*)`, `SUM(...)`, and `GROUP BY` aggregate only the rows that survive RLS, and they pay the cost of reaching that filtered input.

There is no single PostgreSQL 14 setting that makes RLS cheap for aggregates while preserving the same row visibility. Practical mitigations are:

1. **Make the policy predicate cheap and indexable.** Because RLS quals become `baserestrictinfo`, ordinary index path generation can match them to index and bitmap paths [indxpath.c#create_index_paths](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/path/indxpath.c#L198-L336). For tenant-style policies, keep the tenant/security key in a structurally indexable expression and avoid per-row functions or policy subqueries unless the security model requires them. The partial-index prover also includes `baserestrictinfo`: an RLS restriction can help prove a partial-index predicate, while an implication it cannot prove simply fails to help; added restrictions do not make an already-proven partial index cease to be `predOK` [indxpath.c#check_index_predicates](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/path/indxpath.c#L3306-L3421).
2. **Use genuinely leakproof, indexable forms for selective user filters where the operators already provide that property.** Security-level rules prevent a higher-level leaky user qual from becoming an early index qual, while a leakproof or otherwise safely leveled clause can be promoted [optimizer README#security-level-constraints](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/README#L899-L978) [restrictinfo.c#restriction_is_securely_promotable](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/util/restrictinfo.c#L396-L409). `LEAKPROOF` is a security guarantee and superuser-only, not a label to add merely for speed [create_function.sgml#LEAKPROOF](../../../raw/postgres-14/doc/src/sgml/ref/create_function.sgml#L356-L378).
3. **Use partitioning when the security key is also the pruning key.** Plan-time pruning gathers quals from `baserestrictinfo`, so an RLS tenant predicate can remove irrelevant partitions before the aggregate consumes rows [partprune.c#prune_append_rel_partitions](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/partitioning/partprune.c#L742-L804). For partitioned aggregate workloads, `enable_partitionwise_aggregate` lets the planner consider partitionwise aggregation when grouping sets are absent; it is `PGC_USERSET`, so it has session/transaction scope and needs no restart or reload [guc.c#enable_partitionwise_aggregate](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/misc/guc.c#L1108-L1117) [planner.c#partitionwise-aggregate](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/planner.c#L3378-L3408).
4. **Keep policy functions parallel-safe when that declaration is true.** Parallel-hazard analysis walks RLS `securityQuals`, and a parallel-unsafe policy function prevents parallel mode for the query [planner.c#max_parallel_hazard](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/planner.c#L315-L350) [nodeFuncs.c#range_table_entry_walker](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/nodes/nodeFuncs.c#L2483-L2533). The declaration must match the function's real behavior; it is not merely an aggregate tuning switch [clauses.c#parallel-hazard](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/util/clauses.c#L675-L778).
5. **Reduce repeated planning, but do not expect this to remove per-tuple checks.** Prepared statements and PL/pgSQL can reuse RLS-rewritten trees and generic plans while the guarded environment remains valid, as described in [RLS and the plan cache](#rls-and-the-plan-cache). That mitigates repeated planning for aggregate statements; it does not remove executor policy evaluation [plancache.c#RevalidateCachedQuery](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L600-L607) [execMain.c#ExecWithCheckOptions](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/execMain.c#L1977-L2105).
6. **Pre-aggregate only when the result has its own security boundary.** If users should see aggregates over rows they cannot individually see, that is not the same query under RLS. A maintained summary table or trusted reporting API therefore needs its own access checks. PostgreSQL's bypass rules are explicit: superusers and `BYPASSRLS` roles bypass RLS, table owners usually bypass it unless `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` applies, and `SECURITY DEFINER` functions run under the function owner's user id [rls.c#check_enable_rls](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/misc/rls.c#L80-L118) [fmgr.c#fmgr_security_definer](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/fmgr/fmgr.c#L656-L772). This design changes the security boundary; it is not a transparent performance switch.
7. **Do not use `row_security = off` as an optimization for subject users.** `row_security` is `PGC_USERSET` (session/transaction scoped, no restart or reload) [guc.c#row_security](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/misc/guc.c#L1722-L1730), but when a non-bypass user would be affected by filtering, `check_enable_rls()` raises an error instead of bypassing RLS [rls.c#row_security-off](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/misc/rls.c#L120-L132).

One parser boundary needs precision. A direct aggregate or grouping operation at the policy expression's own query level is rejected [parse_agg.c#EXPR_KIND_POLICY](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/parser/parse_agg.c#L330-L346) [parse_agg.c#policy-error](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/parser/parse_agg.c#L398-L407), and the regression suite covers direct `USING (max(c))` failure [rowsecurity.sql#policy-aggregate-rejected](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/test/regress/sql/rowsecurity.sql#L1496-L1503) [rowsecurity.out#policy-aggregate-rejected](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/test/regress/expected/rowsecurity.out#L3568-L3578). Policy expressions may contain sublinks, however, and a sub-SELECT is analyzed in its own child parse state, so an aggregate inside that subquery is possible [parse_expr.c#policy-sublink](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/parser/parse_expr.c#L1688-L1734) [parse_expr.c#parse_sub_analyze](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/parser/parse_expr.c#L1792-L1797) [analyze.c#parse_sub_analyze](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/parser/analyze.c#L181-L202). It carries the planning, execution, concurrency, and snapshot considerations of a policy subquery; it is not a free way to make aggregation cheaper.

### RLS and MultiXact

RLS does **not inherently** make a protected-row filter a MultiXact operation. Core RLS rewrite only attaches `USING` expressions to `securityQuals` and write expressions to `WithCheckOption` lists [rowsecurity.c overview](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L1-L34) [rewriteHandler.c#fireRIRrules-RLS](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rewriteHandler.c#L2256-L2349). A MultiXact is instead tuple-lock storage: a tuple's `xmax` can identify members with `ForKeyShare`, `ForShare`, `ForNoKeyUpdate`, `ForUpdate`, and update statuses [multixact.h#MultiXactStatus](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/include/access/multixact.h#L37-L64).

Two RLS-adjacent paths can nevertheless create or amplify MultiXact work.

#### Policy subqueries that explicitly lock lookup rows

A policy can contain a sub-`SELECT`. The v14 documentation warns that reading another table under `READ COMMITTED` can race with a concurrent security-metadata update, and proposes `SELECT ... FOR SHARE` as one mitigation; it also warns that heavy concurrent row-share locking can be a performance problem when the referenced table is updated frequently [ddl.sgml#RLS-policy-race](../../../raw/postgres-14/doc/src/sgml/ddl.sgml#L2563-L2655) [ddl.sgml#RLS-row-share-lock](../../../raw/postgres-14/doc/src/sgml/ddl.sgml#L2658-L2675).

That lock is not abstract. `ExecLockRows()` maps a `ROW_MARK_SHARE` to `LockTupleShare` and calls the table AM's `table_tuple_lock()` [nodeLockRows.c#ExecLockRows](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/nodeLockRows.c#L160-L190). For heap tables, the call reaches `heap_lock_tuple()`, which sets the backend's oldest-member marker and calls `compute_new_xmax_infomask()` [heapam_handler.c#heapam_tuple_lock](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/access/heap/heapam_handler.c#L347-L367) [heapam.c#heap_lock_tuple](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/access/heap/heapam.c#L5091-L5111). A live existing MultiXact can be expanded with `MultiXactIdExpand()`, while a concurrent single locker or updater can be combined with the new lock through `MultiXactIdCreate()` [heapam.c#compute_new_xmax_infomask](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/access/heap/heapam.c#L5333-L5495). How often the locking subquery runs follows ordinary sublink planning: a correlated subplan can run at every evaluated outer tuple, while an eligible uncorrelated scalar sublink can become an InitPlan [subselect.c#build_subplan](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/subselect.c#L375-L410) [subselect.c#correlated-SubPlan](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/subselect.c#L487-L539).

#### Foreign-key validation fallback

1. `validateForeignKeyConstraint()` first tries `RI_Initial_Check()`, a single bulk validation query. If that returns false, it scans every referencing-table row and calls `RI_FKey_check_ins()` for each row [tablecmds.c#validateForeignKeyConstraint](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/tablecmds.c#L11367-L11415).
2. `RI_Initial_Check()` returns false when either participating table has RLS and the current user is neither `BYPASSRLS` nor owner of each RLS-enabled table involved [ri_triggers.c#RI_Initial_Check](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/adt/ri_triggers.c#L1358-L1368).
3. A row-level FK check can return early for NULL key values; otherwise its lookup query uses `FOR KEY SHARE OF x`, and a matching referenced row reaches the heap tuple-lock/MultiXact path above [ri_triggers.c#RI_FKey_check](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/adt/ri_triggers.c#L330-L390).

RLS therefore does not create a MultiXact for every protected row. The risks are conditional: policy authors may deliberately add locking subqueries, and RLS can turn initial FK validation from one bulk query into many row checks. Ordinary child-table `INSERT`/`UPDATE` FK checks use `FOR KEY SHARE` regardless of RLS; RLS is relevant to that second path because it disables the validation shortcut for affected roles [ri_triggers.c#RI_FKey_check](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/adt/ri_triggers.c#L356-L390) [tablecmds.c#validateForeignKeyConstraint](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/tablecmds.c#L11367-L11415).

Once a tuple's `xmax` is a MultiXact, member lookup first checks a transaction-local cache capped at 256 entries [multixact.c#cache-purpose](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/access/transam/multixact.c#L296-L324) [multixact.c#mXactCachePut](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/access/transam/multixact.c#L1713-L1758). A miss makes `GetMultiXactIdMembers()` read the offsets and members SLRUs [multixact.c#GetMultiXactIdMembers](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/access/transam/multixact.c#L1386-L1580). PostgreSQL 14 has 8 offset buffers and 16 member buffers [multixact.h#buffers](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/include/access/multixact.h#L32-L34). If a required SLRU page is absent from memory, the path can perform `pg_pread` under wait event `SLRURead`; recovery can instead zero-fill a missing file before that I/O point [slru.c#SimpleLruReadPage-IO](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/access/transam/slru.c#L439-L457) [slru.c#SlruPhysicalReadPage](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/access/transam/slru.c#L695-L729) [wait_event.c#SLRURead](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/activity/wait_event.c#L645-L646).

For the full MultiXact mechanism, see [How MultiXact Works in PostgreSQL 14, and How Foreign Keys and Other Operations Degrade Performance When the Local Cache Spills to Secondary Storage](multixact-foreign-keys-cache-spill.md).

### RLS and the plan cache

**Yes. RLS-aware query-tree and plan reuse uses PostgreSQL's general plan cache, guarded by the effective role OID and `row_security` value; a separate relcache entry caches the table's role-independent policy definitions.** The guard detects role-identity and GUC changes but, in this v14 pin, misses authorization changes that keep the same role OID [plancache.c#RevalidateCachedQuery](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L600-L607) [plancache.c#InitPlanCache](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L126-L141). Two independent caches matter here:

- **The relcache policy set (`rd_rsdesc`)** caches each table's *parsed* policies. `RelationBuildRowSecurity()` deserializes every `polqual`/`polwithcheck` node tree and reparents the descriptor under `CacheMemoryContext`, so later queries reuse those trees until relcache invalidation requires a rebuild [policy.c#RelationBuildRowSecurity](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/policy.c#L195-L325). It is **role-independent**: it stores every policy on the table, and role/command filtering happens later at rewrite time, so `SET ROLE` does not invalidate it. A relcache rebuild even keeps the old descriptor when the policy set is unchanged (`equalRSDesc` -> `keep_policies`) [relcache.c#keep_policies](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/relcache.c#L2668-L2725).
- **The plan cache (`CachedPlanSource`/`CachedPlan`)** caches the *derived* security quals and the finished plan, which are specific to the role and `row_security` value in effect when they were built. This is the "special caching for RLS," and the rest of this section is about it.

#### How the RLS plan cache works

A `CachedPlanSource` carries three RLS-specific fields (`rewriteRoleId`, `rewriteRowSecurity`, `dependsOnRLS`) and each derived `CachedPlan` carries `planRoleId` and `dependsOnRole` [plancache.h#CachedPlanSource](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/include/utils/plancache.h#L117-L119) [plancache.h#CachedPlan](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/include/utils/plancache.h#L154-L155). They are wired together as follows:

1. **Rewrite tags the query.** When `get_row_security_policies()` reports an RLS dependency, including an environment-dependent bypass that added no quals, `fireRIRrules()` sets `Query->hasRowSecurity` [rowsecurity.c#RLS_NONE_ENV](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L136-L155) [rewriteHandler.c#hasRowSecurity](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rewriteHandler.c#L2351-L2358).
2. **Dependency extraction records it.** `extract_query_dependencies()` walks the rewritten tree and OR-collects every `hasRowSecurity` flag into the plansource's `dependsOnRLS` [setrefs.c#extract_query_dependencies](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/setrefs.c#L3142-L3167) [setrefs.c#hasRowSecurity](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/setrefs.c#L3219-L3221).
3. **The environment is captured.** At the moment the tree is cached, the plansource records what it was rewritten under: `rewriteRoleId = GetUserId()` and `rewriteRowSecurity = row_security` [plancache.c#capture](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L396-L403), and again after any re-analysis [plancache.c#recapture](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L755-L757).
4. **Reuse is gated on the environment.** Every `GetCachedPlan()` first calls `RevalidateCachedQuery()`, which invalidates the cached query tree when the query depends on RLS and either the user id or `row_security` has changed since capture [plancache.c#RevalidateCachedQuery](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L600-L607).
5. **The plan is tagged role-specific.** `BuildCachedPlan()` sets `plan->planRoleId = GetUserId()` and `plan->dependsOnRole = plansource->dependsOnRLS` [plancache.c#BuildCachedPlan](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L976-L994). A cached *generic* plan is then discarded by `CheckCachedPlan()` when the current user id differs from `planRoleId` [plancache.c#CheckCachedPlan](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L814-L819).

Net effect: a prepared or PL/pgSQL statement over an RLS table can reuse its cached query tree and, when the generic-plan path wins, its cached generic plan while the guarded environment matches and no ordinary dependency, search-path, or relation invalidation rejects reuse [plancache.c#RevalidateCachedQuery](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L568-L778) [plancache.c#CheckCachedPlan](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L787-L865). Custom-plan selection can still rerun the planner over the cached rewritten tree. Unchanged role OID and GUC values also permit reuse across same-role membership, inheritance, bypass, superuser, or database-owner changes even though those inputs can change policy selection or RLS bypass [acl.c#roles_is_member_of](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/adt/acl.c#L4710-L4828) [aclchk.c#has_bypassrls_privilege](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/catalog/aclchk.c#L5471-L5488) [plancache.h#RLS-cache-fields](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/include/utils/plancache.h#L110-L127).

#### In what scenarios does it not work

These are the main reuse boundaries and extra validation/planning costs. Some force re-analysis or replanning; others merely prevent a cheaper reuse path:

- **The role changes.** `SET ROLE`, `SET SESSION AUTHORIZATION`, and entering a `SECURITY DEFINER` function all change `GetUserId()` [miscinit.c#GetUserId](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/init/miscinit.c#L477-L481) — `SET ROLE` assigns through `SetCurrentRoleId()` [variable.c#assign_role](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/variable.c#L955-L961) [miscinit.c#SetCurrentRoleId](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/init/miscinit.c#L905-L942), `SET SESSION AUTHORIZATION` assigns through `SetSessionAuthorization()` [variable.c#assign_session_authorization](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/variable.c#L840-L850) [miscinit.c#SetSessionAuthorization](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/init/miscinit.c#L873-L888), and a `SECURITY DEFINER` call uses `SetUserIdAndSecContext()` inside `fmgr_security_definer()` [fmgr.c#fmgr_security_definer](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/fmgr/fmgr.c#L716-L718). An RLS-dependent cached query tree is re-analyzed when the effective user at reuse differs from the captured user, and a role-dependent generic plan is rejected on a user mismatch [plancache.c#RevalidateCachedQuery](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L600-L607) [plancache.c#CheckCachedPlan](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L814-L819). A pool that executes the same prepared statement under changing roles can therefore rebuild repeatedly.
- **Authorization changes while the effective role OID stays the same.** Policy matching depends on role-membership grants, `rolinherit`, and implicit database-owner membership, while bypass depends on `rolsuper` and `rolbypassrls` [rowsecurity.c#check_role_for_policy](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L771-L792) [acl.c#roles_is_member_of](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/adt/acl.c#L4710-L4828) [aclchk.c#has_bypassrls_privilege](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/catalog/aclchk.c#L5471-L5488). Authorization invalidations clear the corresponding membership caches, but `CachedPlanSource` has no authorization generation and the plan cache registers no auth/database callback [acl.c#RoleMembershipCacheCallback](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/adt/acl.c#L4654-L4707) [plancache.h#RLS-cache-fields](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/include/utils/plancache.h#L110-L127) [plancache.c#InitPlanCache](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L126-L141). A cached statement can therefore keep an old policy set or bypass decision after a role-membership `GRANT`/`REVOKE`, `INHERIT`/`NOINHERIT`, `BYPASSRLS`/`NOBYPASSRLS`, `SUPERUSER`/`NOSUPERUSER`, an intermediate-role inheritance change, or a database-owner change. Recycle affected sessions or run `DISCARD PLANS` before trusting the new authorization state [discard.c#DISCARD-PLANS](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/discard.c#L27-L41).
- **`row_security` differs at reuse.** It is a `PGC_USERSET` boolean (session/transaction scope; no restart or reload needed) [guc.c#row_security](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/misc/guc.c#L1722-L1730). An RLS-dependent tree is invalidated when its captured value differs from the value at the next reuse; changing it away and back before reuse does not meet that comparison [plancache.c#RevalidateCachedQuery](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L600-L607).
- **The fast "simple validity" revalidation path never applies.** `CachedPlanAllowsSimpleValidityCheck()` returns false as soon as `dependsOnRLS` or `dependsOnRole` is set [plancache.c#simple-validity](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L1338-L1348). That fast path is designed for lock-free, table-free generic plans: it separately rejects a query with any range table, CTE, or sublink and a finished plan containing an `RTE_RELATION` [plancache.c#simple-validity-table-checks](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L1350-L1387). An RLS query necessarily has a protected relation, so it requires the full cached-plan validation path rather than the cheap PL/pgSQL-oriented flag check.
- **Ad-hoc (simple-protocol) queries are never plan-cached at all.** `exec_simple_query()` runs analysis/rewrite and planning directly, with no `CachedPlanSource`, so a directly issued `SELECT` repeats those stages [postgres.c#exec_simple_query](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/tcop/postgres.c#L1098-L1102). SPI can explicitly construct a one-shot cached plan; one-shot plans are always custom, cannot be saved, and therefore supply no reusable generic plan [spi.c#one-shot-plan](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/executor/spi.c#L2335-L2377) [plancache.c#SaveCachedPlan](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L443-L467) [plancache.c#choose_custom_plan](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L1027-L1029).
- **Parameterized statements often re-plan for their first executions.** Under `plan_cache_mode = auto`, after the one-shot, bound-parameter, cursor, and forced-plan checks, the first five custom-plan opportunities build fresh custom plans; custom planning can continue when its average cost wins [plancache.c#choose_custom_plan](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L1027-L1069). Each custom build reruns `pg_plan_queries()`, including RLS qual ordering, but can reuse the cached rewritten tree while its guard remains valid.
- **`plan_cache_mode` can force the generic/custom choice.** It is a `PGC_USERSET` enum (session/transaction scope; no restart or reload needed) with `auto`, `force_generic_plan`, and `force_custom_plan` values [guc.c#plan_cache_mode](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/misc/guc.c#L4937-L4946) [guc.c#plan_cache_mode_options](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/misc/guc.c#L491-L495). `choose_custom_plan()` first rules out custom planning for cases where it is not useful, such as statements with no bound parameters, and then checks `plan_cache_mode` before the normal heuristic [plancache.c#choose_custom_plan](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L1031-L1042). For a parameterized RLS prepared statement where custom planning is possible, `force_custom_plan` preserves the cached rewritten tree when the role and `row_security` value are unchanged, but forces a fresh plan build on each execution.
- **SQL `DECLARE CURSOR` is not an additional cached-plan reuse surface.** `PerformCursorOpen()` rewrites and plans the cursor query immediately, copies the plan tree into the portal, and defines the portal with no `CachedPlan` reference [portalcmds.c#PerformCursorOpen](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/portalcmds.c#L75-L110). `PortalDefineQuery()` records a cached-plan reference only when its caller passes one, and prepared-statement execution is the path that passes the `CachedPlan` from `GetCachedPlan()` [portalmem.c#PortalDefineQuery](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/mmgr/portalmem.c#L265-L302) [prepare.c#ExecuteQuery](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/prepare.c#L229-L242). A `WITH HOLD` cursor survives by copying rows into a temporary memory/file tuplestore, not by reusing a plan across transactions [portalcmds.c#PersistHoldablePortal](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/portalcmds.c#L308-L314) [declare.sgml#WITH-HOLD](../../../raw/postgres-14/doc/src/sgml/ref/declare.sgml#L203-L213).
- **Policy or table DDL invalidates the caches.** Policy create, alter, and drop explicitly invalidate the relation cache, while the table-flag paths update `pg_class` [policy.c#CreatePolicy-invalidation](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/policy.c#L719-L753) [policy.c#AlterPolicy-invalidation](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/policy.c#L1082-L1087) [policy.c#RemovePolicyById](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/policy.c#L385-L399) [tablecmds.c#ATExecSetRowSecurity](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/tablecmds.c#L15835-L15883). The relcache rebuild retains `rd_rsdesc` when `equalRSDesc()` finds no policy change; a changed or destroyed descriptor is freed. `PlanCacheRelCallback()` invalidates dependent saved queries/plans [relcache.c#rd_rsdesc-free](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/relcache.c#L2463-L2467) [relcache.c#keep_policies](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/relcache.c#L2669-L2725) [plancache.c#PlanCacheRelCallback](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L1945-L2006). The next protected use rebuilds the affected layers as needed.

#### The performance overhead of having no cache

When an environment or dependency mismatch invalidates a cached query tree, `RevalidateCachedQuery()` re-runs parse analysis and rewriting via `pg_analyze_and_rewrite()`, and `BuildCachedPlan()` runs `pg_plan_queries()` [plancache.c#reanalyze](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L637-L699) [plancache.c#replan](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L936-L940). An uncached simple-protocol query performs those stages directly on every execution [postgres.c#exec_simple_query](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/tcop/postgres.c#L1098-L1102). For an RLS table, repeated work includes:

- **Re-deriving the security quals** — `get_row_security_policies()` re-selects the matching permissive/restrictive policies for the current command and role and rebuilds the OR/AND qual trees and `WITH CHECK` options [rowsecurity.c#add_security_quals](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rowsecurity.c#L558-L637).
- **Re-locking and re-expanding policy sublinks** — if any applicable policy contains a subquery, the rewriter re-locks the referenced relations and re-fires RIR (view) rules on them [rewriteHandler.c#policy-sublinks](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/rewrite/rewriteHandler.c#L2285-L2336).
- **Re-ordering quals for leakproofness** — the planner re-computes `qual_security_level` and re-runs `order_qual_clauses()`, so RLS quals still precede non-leakproof user quals [createplan.c#order_qual_clauses](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/createplan.c#L5191-L5300).

Two mitigations are visible in source. First, a role/`row_security`-only re-analysis can reuse the still-valid, role-independent `rd_rsdesc`, so it repeats policy selection and planning without re-reading and deserializing `pg_policy`; policy DDL or another relcache invalidation can separately force the descriptor rebuild [policy.c#RelationBuildRowSecurity](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/policy.c#L195-L325) [relcache.c#keep_policies](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/relcache.c#L2668-L2725). Second, a plain non-RLS query pays none of this: `check_enable_rls()` returns `RLS_NONE` for a table without `relrowsecurity`, so `dependsOnRLS` is never set and the ordinary (non-RLS) caching applies [rls.c#check_enable_rls](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/misc/rls.c#L61-L78). The pinned tests measure none of these costs; see [Test coverage](#test-coverage) and [Open Questions](#open-questions) [rowsecurity.sql#plan-shape](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/test/regress/sql/rowsecurity.sql#L133-L168).

### Settings related to the feature

The pinned core GUC table defines `row_security` as the server setting specific to RLS [guc.c#row_security](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/misc/guc.c#L1722-L1730). `plan_cache_mode` and `enable_partitionwise_aggregate` are general planner GUCs relevant to the cache and aggregation follow-ups. The other controls below are catalog DDL, role attributes, policy clauses, extension hooks, inspection surfaces, and client or replication options.

#### `row_security` (GUC)

`row_security` is a Boolean `PGC_USERSET` GUC in the `CLIENT_CONN_STATEMENT` group, defaulting to **on** [guc.c#row_security](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/misc/guc.c#L1722-L1730) [rls.h#row_security](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/include/utils/rls.h#L17). `PGC_USERSET` means **session/transaction scope**: any user can use `SET` or `SET LOCAL`, with no server reload or restart. `off` does not bypass filtering for a subject role; when RLS would apply and the caller used `check_enable_rls(..., noError = false)`, it raises an error. Superusers and `BYPASSRLS` roles bypass before that check, while owners bypass unless `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` applies, apart from the owner-only no-force RI context [rls.c#check_enable_rls](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/misc/rls.c#L80-L132). `pg_dump` sets the GUC off by default so a subject role fails rather than silently omitting rows [pg_dump.c#row_security](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/bin/pg_dump/pg_dump.c#L1226-L1233) [config.sgml#row_security](../../../raw/postgres-14/doc/src/sgml/config.sgml#L8226-L8234).

#### `plan_cache_mode` (GUC)

`plan_cache_mode` is not RLS-specific, but it directly controls the generic-vs-custom plan choice described in [RLS and the plan cache](#rls-and-the-plan-cache). It is a `PGC_USERSET` enum in the `QUERY_TUNING_OTHER` group, defaulting to `auto`, with `force_generic_plan` and `force_custom_plan` alternatives [guc.c#plan_cache_mode](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/misc/guc.c#L4937-L4946) [guc.c#plan_cache_mode_options](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/misc/guc.c#L491-L495). Because the context is `PGC_USERSET`, it is **session/transaction scoped**: any user can change it with `SET plan_cache_mode = ...` or `SET LOCAL`, and it needs no server restart or reload. For prepared statements where custom planning is possible, `force_custom_plan` makes each execution build a fresh custom plan, while `force_generic_plan` makes `choose_custom_plan()` take the generic path when planning is needed [plancache.c#choose_custom_plan](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L1031-L1042) [config.sgml#plan_cache_mode](../../../raw/postgres-14/doc/src/sgml/config.sgml#L5903-L5916). RLS-dependent generic plans still remain guarded by role and `row_security` revalidation [plancache.c#CheckCachedPlan](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L814-L819) [plancache.c#RevalidateCachedQuery](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/cache/plancache.c#L600-L607).

#### General performance-GUC boundary

`enable_partitionwise_aggregate`, discussed in the aggregation mitigation, is another general `PGC_USERSET` planner GUC. It defaults to `off`, has session/transaction scope with no restart or reload, and only enables consideration of partitionwise aggregate/grouping plans; it does not enable, disable, or bypass RLS [guc.c#enable_partitionwise_aggregate](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/misc/guc.c#L1107-L1116) [planner.c#partitionwise-aggregate](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/optimizer/plan/planner.c#L3378-L3408). Other ordinary planner method/cost GUCs can affect the plan chosen for any query, including one with RLS quals, but they are not RLS feature settings; this page does not relabel the entire planner configuration surface as RLS-specific.

#### Table-level settings (`ALTER TABLE`)

Two `pg_class` booleans control RLS per table, both set through `ALTER TABLE`:

| Statement | Catalog effect | Set by |
|---|---|---|
| `ENABLE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` / `DISABLE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` | `relrowsecurity = true/false` | [tablecmds.c#ATExecSetRowSecurity](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/tablecmds.c#L15835-L15857) |
| `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` / `NO FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` | `relforcerowsecurity = true/false` | [tablecmds.c#ATExecForceNoForceRowSecurity](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/tablecmds.c#L15862-L15883) |

The four spellings are distinct `ALTER TABLE` subcommands in the grammar [gram.y#alter-table-rls](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/parser/gram.y#L2659-L2685). They require ownership, accept ordinary or partitioned tables, reject protected system catalogs, do not recurse to children, and acquire `AccessExclusiveLock`; these are catalog changes, not restart/reload settings [tablecmds.c#RLS-lock-mode](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/tablecmds.c#L4266-L4284) [tablecmds.c#RLS-no-recursion](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/tablecmds.c#L4781-L4794) [tablecmds.c#ATSimplePermissions](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/tablecmds.c#L6060-L6113). `DISABLE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` leaves `pg_policy` rows in place but ignores them, while `FORCE` subjects the owner to RLS except in the no-force RI context [ddl.sgml#disable-RLS](../../../raw/postgres-14/doc/src/sgml/ddl.sgml#L2381-L2386) [rls.c#owner](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/misc/rls.c#L90-L118).

#### Role-level setting (`BYPASSRLS`)

The `BYPASSRLS` / `NOBYPASSRLS` role attribute maps to `pg_authid.rolbypassrls` [pg_authid.h#rolbypassrls](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/include/catalog/pg_authid.h#L41). The parser turns those spellings into a `bypassrls` option [gram.y#bypassrls](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/parser/gram.y#L1156-L1159). Only a superuser may create a role with `BYPASSRLS` or change an existing role's `BYPASSRLS` state; a `CREATEROLE` user can create an ordinary `NOBYPASSRLS` role [user.c#CreateRole-bypassrls](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/user.c#L285-L315) [user.c#AlterRole-bypassrls](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/user.c#L711-L737). Superusers always bypass RLS even without the attribute [aclchk.c#has_bypassrls_privilege](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/catalog/aclchk.c#L5471-L5488).

#### Per-policy options (`CREATE POLICY` / `ALTER POLICY`)

`CREATE POLICY name ON table` accepts, in order, the per-policy "settings" that become `pg_policy` columns [gram.y#CreatePolicyStmt](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/parser/gram.y#L5237-L5252):

- `AS PERMISSIVE | RESTRICTIVE` — **default `PERMISSIVE`** [gram.y#permissive-default](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/parser/gram.y#L5288-L5304).
- `FOR ALL | SELECT | INSERT | UPDATE | DELETE` — **default `ALL`** [gram.y#cmd-default](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/parser/gram.y#L5306-L5317).
- `TO role[, ...]` — **default `PUBLIC`** [gram.y#to-default](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/parser/gram.y#L5278-L5281); a `PUBLIC` entry mixed with named roles warns and collapses to `PUBLIC` alone [policy.c#policy_role_list_to_array](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/policy.c#L139-L186).
- `USING (expr)` — the existing-row visibility/modification filter [gram.y#using](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/parser/gram.y#L5268-L5271).
- `WITH CHECK (expr)` — the new-row write check [gram.y#with-check](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/parser/gram.y#L5273-L5276). For `ALL` and `UPDATE`, a missing `WITH CHECK` falls back to `USING`; `INSERT` permits only `WITH CHECK`, while `SELECT` and `DELETE` permit only `USING` [policy.c#policy-expression-rules](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/policy.c#L597-L616) [create_policy.sgml#WITH-CHECK-fallback](../../../raw/postgres-14/doc/src/sgml/ref/create_policy.sgml#L84-L98).

`ALTER POLICY` can change only the roles, `USING`, and `WITH CHECK`; it has no `AS` or `FOR` clause, so a policy's permissive/restrictive nature and command cannot be altered after creation [gram.y#AlterPolicyStmt](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/parser/gram.y#L5254-L5266). Policy create, alter, rename, and drop take `AccessExclusiveLock`; they require table ownership and reject relation kinds other than ordinary and partitioned tables, plus protected system catalogs [policy.c#RangeVarCallbackForPolicy](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/policy.c#L56-L99) [objectaddress.c#DROP-POLICY-ownership](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/catalog/objectaddress.c#L2362-L2385) [policy.c#CreatePolicy-lock](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/policy.c#L631-L638) [policy.c#AlterPolicy-lock](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/policy.c#L808-L814) [policy.c#RemovePolicyById-lock](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/policy.c#L363-L399) [policy.c#rename_policy-lock](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/policy.c#L1113-L1119). These are catalog DDL operations, not restart/reload settings.

#### Extension hooks

Not user settings, but configurable behavior: `row_security_policy_hook_permissive` and `row_security_policy_hook_restrictive` let a loaded module add policies [rowsecurity.h#hooks](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/include/rewrite/rowsecurity.h#L37-L42). Each hook is one global function pointer; the in-tree test module saves the previous values, installs its callbacks, and restores the prior pointers on unload [test_rls_hooks.c#hook-installation](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/test/modules/test_rls_hooks/test_rls_hooks.c#L30-L58).

#### SQL, catalog, and `psql` inspection

The `row_security_active(oid)` and `row_security_active(text)` SQL functions call `check_enable_rls(..., noError = true)` and report whether RLS is active in the caller's current environment without raising the `row_security = off` error [rls.c#row_security_active](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/utils/misc/rls.c#L135-L167) [pg_proc.dat#row_security_active](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/include/catalog/pg_proc.dat#L11450-L11458). `pg_policies` exposes decoded policy definitions, `pg_tables.rowsecurity` exposes the enable flag, and `pg_roles.rolbypassrls` exposes the non-secret role attribute [system_views.sql#pg_policies](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/catalog/system_views.sql#L73-L106) [system_views.sql#pg_tables](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/catalog/system_views.sql#L127-L139) [system_views.sql#pg_roles](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/catalog/system_views.sql#L17-L33). `psql` table description reads the table flags and policy catalog, then labels enabled, forced, disabled, and no-policy states and prints command, roles, `USING`, and `WITH CHECK` [describe.c#RLS-policy-display](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/bin/psql/describe.c#L2840-L2915). These are inspection surfaces, not settings, and require no restart or reload.

#### Backup and logical-replication controls

`pg_dump --enable-row-security` is a client option that makes the dump session set `row_security = on` and dump only rows visible to the dump role. Without it, `pg_dump` sets the session GUC off so a subject role errors instead of producing an incomplete dump [pg_dump.c#row-security-setting](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/bin/pg_dump/pg_dump.c#L1226-L1233) [pg_dump.sgml#enable-row-security](../../../raw/postgres-14/doc/src/sgml/ref/pg_dump.sgml#L754-L772). `pg_restore --enable-row-security` likewise emits `SET row_security = on`; otherwise restore output sets it off. RLS-enabled restore requires INSERT-format data because `COPY FROM` is rejected when RLS applies [pg_backup_archiver.c#row-security-setting](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/bin/pg_dump/pg_backup_archiver.c#L3219-L3223) [pg_restore.sgml#enable-row-security](../../../raw/postgres-14/doc/src/sgml/ref/pg_restore.sgml#L570-L589). Both are client/session controls and need no server reload or restart.

For logical replication, the publisher connection's initial table synchronization issues `COPY table TO STDOUT`, so it can execute publisher-side RLS policy expressions [tablesync.c#copy_table](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/replication/logical/tablesync.c#L836-L890) [copy.c#RLS](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/copy.c#L201-L227). PostgreSQL 14 documents connection option `options=-crow_security=off` for an untrusted-owner environment; it makes policy applicability halt that publisher read instead of filtering rows [logical-replication.sgml#security](../../../raw/postgres-14/doc/src/sgml/logical-replication.sgml#L533-L562). Ongoing `pgoutput` sends decoded WAL tuple changes rather than querying each row through RLS [pgoutput.c#pgoutput_change](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/replication/pgoutput/pgoutput.c#L573-L680). On the subscriber, workers connect as the subscription owner, and PostgreSQL 14 requires both a creator and a replacement owner to be superuser; apply therefore runs under an RLS-bypassing role [launcher.c#subscription-owner](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/replication/logical/launcher.c#L848-L868) [worker.c#worker-connection](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/replication/logical/worker.c#L3110-L3117) [subscriptioncmds.c#create-owner](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/subscriptioncmds.c#L368-L380) [subscriptioncmds.c#new-owner](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/backend/commands/subscriptioncmds.c#L1389-L1402).

## Open Questions

- This page does not provide a quantified throughput or latency benchmark by policy count, role count, estimator error, parallel-safety declaration, aggregate shape, partition count, plan-cache churn, locking policy subquery, or RLS-induced FK workload. The core and hook suites use untimed result/error checks plus `EXPLAIN (COSTS OFF)`, not `EXPLAIN (ANALYZE)` or timing primitives [rowsecurity.sql#plan-shape](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/test/regress/sql/rowsecurity.sql#L133-L168) [test_rls_hooks.sql#plan-shape](../../../raw/postgres-14/src/test/modules/test_rls_hooks/sql/test_rls_hooks.sql#L61-L91). A whole-tree search also found no RLS scenario in the pinned checkout's benchmark/performance directories, so measurements remain workload-specific and external to this evidence base.
- The exact definitions and volatility declarations for external JWT helper functions such as `auth.uid()` and `auth.jwt()` are not present in the pinned PostgreSQL 14 checkout. The InitPlan behavior is verified for PostgreSQL's planner and executor, but a production policy should verify the helper's volatility and whether first-demand, at-most-once evaluation **per generated InitPlan occurrence** is semantically correct; rewrite copies can create more than one such occurrence from one written policy expression.
- The exact set and minor-release provenance of RLS-related fixes shipped within the 14.x series (the v14 checkout is pinned at `REL_14_23-3-g5c00f4e2e3b`) has not been traced here. This page describes the behavior as it stands at the pin, not its change history.
