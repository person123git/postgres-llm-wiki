The extreme case, the same 6.75 GB heap with `vacuum_truncate = off` and **zero rows**, still read all 884,956 blocks to produce a **0-byte** new heap. Four runs agree byte-for-byte on `heap_blks_read`, `rchar` (7,250,034,354), `syscr` (885,019) and WAL (84,720); a warm page cache changed only `read_bytes` (0) and the clock. Also measured: **885 `XLOG/FPI` records for 885 new pages** under `pg_waldump` (7,377,944 bytes at `replica` against 84,720 at `minimal`), `heap_blks_scanned` reporting 0 of 884,956 for 49 of 51 samples and for all 94 samples on the rowless heap, an index rebuild that reads only the new 885-block heap with `idx_blks_read` 0, `VACUUM FULL` at 2.05 s against a cost-delayed `VACUUM (DISABLE_PAGE_SKIPPING)` at 57.27 s over identical bytes, the old relfilenode left at 0 bytes with its higher segments already unlinked and removed at `CHECKPOINT`, and 226 of 226 line pointers retained per emptied page (`pd_lower` 928, `pgstattuple` free 88.53% not ~100%). The source analysis behind it: reads scale with the physical file and nothing else: `vacuum_rel` hands `FULL` to `cluster_rel` with `InvalidOid`, so `use_sort` is false and the copy callback opens a plain `SnapshotAny` `table_beginscan` that reads every block once through `ReadBufferExtended`, with zero `visibilitymap` and zero `PrefetchBuffer` references in `cluster.c`, `heapam_handler.c` and `rewriteheap.c`. Writes, WAL (`XLogIsNeeded()` gated, one `log_newpage` FPI per new page), the closing `heap_sync`, and the index rebuilds all scale with the surviving rows instead, and reclaiming the old multi-GB relfilenode is filesystem metadata work — dirty old-heap buffers are dropped unwritten, the first segment is truncated with a deferred unlink, later 1 GB segments are unlinked at once. Adds the `raw_heap_insert` packing rule with a `floor(8168 / (MAXALIGN(t_len) + 4))` capacity derivation, a 4 GB worked example (184 kB to 2.5 MB of writes, roughly 1,700:1 to 22,800:1), the 32-buffer `BAS_BULKREAD` ring and its `StrategyRejectBuffer` escape hatch, hint bits as the only source of old-heap writes, why no `vacuum_delay_point` throttles the rewrite, and the doc-versus-source conflict over "extra disk space approximately equal to the size of the table". The review corrected three substantive claims: `heap_blks_scanned` is written once per *returned tuple* as `rs_cblock + 1` and never squared up at end of scan, so it under-reports exactly this shape and `pg_statio_all_tables.heap_blks_read` is the honest counter; vacuumed-empty pages keep their line pointer arrays, since `PageRepairFragmentation` "doesn't remove unused line pointers", so `PageGetMaxOffsetNumber` is not 0 and `heapgettup` walks the array rejecting entries on `ItemIdIsNormal`; and the two published monitoring statements needed a `BEGIN`/`COMMIT` wrapper, because a top-level `SET LOCAL` only raises `WarnNoTransactionBlock` and leaves the query untimed.
